# A calendar date is not an instant, and "today" is a question about a place

Two bugs that recurring work invites, both silent, both wrong only for some users or only
on some days — the worst combination for noticing.

**Adding a day is not adding 86,400,000 milliseconds.** A local day across a daylight
saving boundary is 23 or 25 hours. Advance by a fixed duration and the date lands on the
previous or next day, and every occurrence after it is shifted. The failure appears twice a
year, in one hemisphere at a time, for users in the affected zones. All arithmetic here
happens in UTC — which has no DST — over `YYYY-MM-DD` components, and a date is only
interpreted in a zone when answering what day it is there.

**"Today" is not a property of the server.** At `2026-03-08T06:30:00Z` it is the 8th in
London and still the 7th in Denver. A generator that uses the server's date creates
tomorrow's todo early for everyone west of it and misses today's for everyone east. The
zone is stored **on the series** and is a required field with no default — a default would
be the server's, and the resulting bug is invisible to whoever deployed it because they are
sitting next to the machine that is right.

The browser knows the answer, so the form sends it:

```ts
timeZone: Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone
```

Asking costs nothing and removes the entire class of bug. `Intl.DateTimeFormat('en-CA')` is
also the one locale that formats as `YYYY-MM-DD`, so converting an instant to a local
calendar date needs no reassembly and no library.

**Test both hemispheres.** A suite that only checks March catches spring-forward and misses
the zones transitioning in April the other way. `packages/scheduling` tests
`America/Denver` and `Australia/Sydney` for exactly that reason.

Related: [[L-025]], where a zone-less timestamp compared against `now()` made the job queue
claim nothing at all. Same family — time that looks like a number and is not.
