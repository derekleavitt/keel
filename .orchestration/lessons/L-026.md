# An over-specified guard fires on improvements as loudly as on regressions

A Phase 0.1 guard asserted `session.expires_at` has SQL type exactly `'timestamp'`. Its
*intent*, stated in its own name, was "not a `date`" — a session expires at a moment, not
on a day.

K-007 correctly gave the column a time zone, and the guard failed. Nothing had regressed;
the thing it was guarding had been improved, and the test could not tell the difference.

That is worse than it sounds. A guard that fires on correct changes teaches people to edit
guards, and an edited guard is one nobody trusts. The reflex it trains — "the test is
wrong, loosen it" — is the same reflex that eventually loosens a guard that was right.

**Assert the property, not the representation.** `toMatch(/^timestamp/)` plus
`not.toBe('date')` says exactly what the test means and survives any change that keeps it
true.

Same shape as the schema-assembly guard that hardcoded `authTables` and broke on the first
new feature: enforcement that does not tolerate correct change decays into decoration.
