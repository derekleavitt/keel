# T-12 — background jobs and email

Keel had no async story at all. Now it has a queue, an email transport, a worker endpoint
and somewhere to look when a job fails. 19 new unit tests; 46 browser tests still green.

## Why Postgres and not a real queue

One fewer service to run, back up and secure — but the property that actually decided it is
that **a job can be enqueued in the same transaction as the write that caused it.** "Saved
the record but lost the email" is the classic background-work bug, and this designs it out
rather than retrying around it. `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` gives safe concurrent claiming
with no broker.

The honest limit is thousands of jobs a minute, not millions, and it is written down beside
the table. When that stops being enough the handler registry and the interface stay; only
the claim mechanism changes.

## Almost every test is a failure path

A queue that runs happy-path jobs is easy. Everything worth having is about what happens
when a handler throws: backoff with a ceiling, a dead letter rather than infinite retry, an
unknown job kind dead-lettering instead of blocking the queue, and one poisonous job not
stopping the ones behind it.

The digest **fans out per user** rather than looping inside one job, for the same reason: a
single job would be one failure away from nobody getting mail, and its retry would re-send
to everyone it had already reached. There is a test that fails exactly one recipient and
asserts the other still gets theirs.

## Two bugs worth the time they cost

**A zone-less timestamp compared against `now()` is silently never due.** `run_at` is
`timestamp`, `now()` is `timestamptz`, and Postgres reconciles them through the server's
zone — so a UTC-stored job is never claimed. No error; the queue simply does nothing and
every test reports zero. The symptom pointed at the claim query, which was fine. Selecting
`run_at <= now() as due` and reading `false` settled it. [[L-025]], and the repo-wide schema
fix is K-007.

**A digest computed "today" from the wall clock.** A job for the 15th, retried on the 16th,
would report every item as overdue — a different email from the one that failed.
`buildAgenda` now takes an optional `asOf` day: pages omit it, scheduled jobs must pass it.

## Development email never sends

Mail is written to `.keel/mail/` as readable files. A real provider with a test key
eventually sends something real to a real person, usually the day someone seeds the
database from production. Production defaults to a transport that *refuses* to send, so an
unconfigured system fails loudly into the dead-letter queue rather than dropping mail
silently.

## A variance problem I would not have predicted

`JobHandler<T>` is contravariant in `T`, so a registry of handlers with different payload
types cannot be `JobHandler<unknown>[]`. `readonly JobHandler<never>[]` is the one
signature that works without `any`, at the cost of a single cast inside `runJobs` where the
payload genuinely is unknown until the handler is chosen by kind.

Worth noting the gate caught my own `noNonNullAssertion` rule being violated in a test I
had just written. The rules apply to me too, which is the point of them being mechanical.
